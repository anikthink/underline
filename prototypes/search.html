Nightstand Search and commands

⌘K opens one box from anywhere in the app. It finds books, your underlines and every command, and what it shows first depends on where you are: the books you're reading in the library, the open book in the Reader, the selection when books are picked. Tab on a book lists everything you can do with it, so no command has to be remembered.

How it behaves

  1. One box, three kinds of result. Books, underlines and commands, grouped and in that order, except that commands for a selection come first. Each group shows a few, with a row to see the rest.
  2. Where you are decides the start. The library offers what you're reading. The Reader scopes to the open book and its reader settings. A selection offers send, mark and edit for those books.
  3. Words match from their start. Every word you type has to begin a word in the result, in any order. That keeps three-letter searches from filling up with noise. Titles rank above authors and shelves, and quotes above notes.
  4. Commands teach their keys. Each command shows its shortcut. Go-to shortcuts are two keys (G then R for Reading), so they never need a modifier.
  5. Enter does the obvious thing. A book opens, or starts if it's Not started. An underline opens in Notes, and ⌘↵ opens the book at its page. A line in the text takes the Reader there.

Decided

  1. Text search covers the open book only. In the Reader, ⌘K searches the whole of the open book's text. Elsewhere it searches titles, authors, shelves, underlines and notes, but not the text inside books, so there's no index to build when books are added.
  2. Keep the G-then-key shortcuts. G then A, R, N, F or U goes to All books, Reading, Not started, Finished or All underlines. The palette shows them next to each place, which is how people learn them.
